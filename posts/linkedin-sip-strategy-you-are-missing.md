# The SIP Strategy You're Missing (LinkedIn post, Synova)

Most people start a SIP and never touch it again.

That's the mistake.

Here's an illustration that changed how I think about SIPs.

Two investors. Same starting SIP. Same 30 years. Same assumed return.

Investor A:
→ ₹1 lakh a month, every month, for 30 years
→ Total invested: ₹3.60 crore
→ Illustrative value at 12% p.a.: about ₹34.95 crore

Looks great on paper, right?

Now look at it after inflation.

At 6% inflation, ₹34.95 crore in 30 years buys roughly what ₹6.08 crore buys today.
And a lifestyle that costs ₹1 lakh a month today could cost about ₹5.74 lakh a month by then.

So your expenses grow every year. Your salary grows every year.
But your SIP stays frozen at the number you picked on day one.

Investor B does one thing differently.

Every year, when the increment comes in, they raise their SIP by 11%.

That's it. We call it the Ladder SIP: one step up, every year, in line with your income.

→ Starts at ₹1 lakh a month
→ Year 2: ₹1.11 lakh. Year 3: ₹1.23 lakh. And so on.
→ Total invested over 30 years: about ₹23.88 crore
→ Illustrative value at the same 12% p.a.: about ₹98.42 crore

To be clear, Investor B also invested a lot more money. That's the whole point.
The gap doesn't come from a magic fund or a higher return assumption. It comes from letting your investments grow at the same pace as your income, instead of letting lifestyle eat the raise.

3 things that make the Ladder SIP work:
1. Tie the step-up to your appraisal month, so it happens on autopilot
2. Pick a step-up % you can actually sustain, even in a slow year (5% beats 15% that you stop after two years)
3. Review it once a year with someone who knows your full picture

Most SIP platforms let you set an annual top-up when you register the mandate. It takes two minutes.
