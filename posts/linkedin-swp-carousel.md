# SWP: Turning Your Corpus into a Monthly Income (LinkedIn caption for the carousel PDF)

---

₹50 lakh can pay you ₹33,333 a month, or ₹16,667 a month and still be worth about ₹1.38 crore 20 years later.

That's the whole idea behind an SWP — a systematic withdrawal plan. You put a lump sum into a mutual fund and take out a fixed amount every month. Whatever you don't withdraw stays invested, so the corpus can keep growing while it pays you.

The one decision you really have to get right is how much you take out each year.

In this carousel I've run the same ₹50 lakh at an illustrative 8% a year, three ways:

→ 4% a year (₹16,667 a month): about ₹1.38 crore left after 20 years
→ 6% a year (₹25,000 a month): about ₹91 lakh left
→ 8% a year (₹33,333 a month): about ₹43 lakh left

At 8%, the withdrawals eat almost all of the growth, so after inflation the money that's left buys a lot less than the ₹50 lakh you started with.

The carousel also covers what the table can't show you: inflation (₹25,000 won't buy the same in 15 years), a weak run of returns in the first few years, and tax on the gains in every unit you redeem.

So the right withdrawal rate comes down to two things: how much income you need, and how many years it has to last.

If you're close to retirement, what monthly amount are you planning around?
